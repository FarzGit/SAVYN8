Sevyn8 Website Brief
5 October 2026
Why this site needs to look different
The problem with every wearable today
Oura gives a readiness score. Whoop gives a recovery score. Gabit's coach talks about the numbers. All three stop at the same wall: you see a score, you feel informed, & then you're on your own. Nobody tells you what to actually do next.
That's the real reason people stop wearing these rings after a few months. The score stops surprising you, nothing in your life changes because of it, & the ring quietly becomes jewelry.
What Sevyn8 does differently
Most products end at telling you something. Sevyn8 is built to go further: once it knows what's going on in your body & has worked out what that actually means, it helps you do something about it.
That last part, actually doing something, is where every other ring on the market goes quiet. A product that can act on your behalf is worth a lot more to you than one that just reports on you.
The ecosystem this opens up
Once something tells you what's wrong & what it means, there's a real next step to connect you to. Here's the full width of what that can cover:
Fitness: trainers, gyms, physiotherapy, recovery & massage, home workout programs
Food: grocery delivery, meal kits, health-focused restaurants, supplement subscriptions
Doctors: telehealth consultations, specialists for whatever the data points to, second opinions
Lab tests: diagnostic labs, home sample collection, imaging, full-body checkup packages
Emergency: ambulance dispatch, ER locators, alerts to an emergency contact when something looks serious
Pharmacy: medicine delivery, prescription refills, supplement reorders
Mental health: therapy & counseling platforms, stress & sleep coaching
Sleep: sleep clinics, CPAP & sleep-aid providers, mattress & sleep-product recommendations
Insurance: wellness-linked discounts tied to a person's own policy
Women's health: fertility & cycle-related care, prenatal support
Elder care: fall detection tied to a caregiver alert, elder care services
Habit coaching: smoking, drinking, stress, built on real data instead of guesswork
Travel health: vaccination & travel medicine before a trip
That's a long list on purpose. The width of it is the point: once something can act, it can act in almost any direction health touches.
Why this is hard to copy
A better score is not hard to copy. Every ring company above already has some version of one.
Acting on the data is a different problem. It needs something that can tell what actually matters out of all the noise, plus real, working relationships with the people on the other end, the doctor, the lab, the pharmacy. That combination takes real work to build. It's not a feature a competitor ships in their next update.
How this shows up on the site
The site should show the whole width of this from day one, not just the ring & not just an app that explains your numbers. The pitch is the whole arc: something tells you what's happening, makes sense of it, & gets you to the right next step, whatever that step turns out to be.
Don't spell this out as a three-step framework on the page. Nobody wants a flowchart in the hero. Say it the way a person would describe it to a friend: this thing actually tells me what to do, not just what my numbers are.
Fitness, food, doctors, labs, emergencies & everything listed above should read as the full promise of the brand, not a limited starter list. Partnerships for each one go live behind that promise as they get signed. The site can make the bigger claim honestly, since it's the real size of where this is going, but what a visitor can actually book on day one should stay true to what's actually working, so the promise doesn't outrun the product. Every section below is built to carry that promise without overstating it.
The site, section by section
Homepage
Hero: an abstract or motion visual, not a photo of the ring & not a screenshot of the app. The headline says what the product does for a person, in plain words. No framework name anywhere on the page. The ring is named directly in the copy, so nobody is confused about what they're buying.
What it does: a short section right under the hero. Something looks at what's happening in your body, makes sense of it in plain language, then points you to a real next step. A few lines. No diagram, no flowchart.
The ring: photos, price, sizes, a working buy button. Every visitor should reach this within one scroll of the homepage.
The companion: what it does & how it reads the ring's data. Real screens once the app is ready, nothing mocked up shown as finished.
The ecosystem: a short preview of where this goes. Fitness, food, doctors, labs, emergency & the rest, grouped into a handful of categories, not a long list. Links through to the full partners page.
Our story: two or three lines on why this exists, linking through to the About page.
Footer: buy link, contact details, social links, legal pages.
Ring product page
Real photos, specs, sizing guide, price, a working buy button. No comparison table against Oura, Whoop, Ultrahuman or Gabit. No medical or health claims anywhere on this page. Wellness language only.
Companion page
Build this page only once the app has real, working screens to show. Content: what it tracks, how it coaches, how it connects to the ring. Do not show mock screens or an unfinished build as if it were done.
Partners & ecosystem page
This is a new page, separate from the homepage preview. Group the partners by what they're for, not as one flat list:
Everyday health: fitness, food, sleep, mental health, habit coaching
Medical: doctors, lab tests, pharmacy, women's health
When it matters most: emergency, elder care
Keeping it covered: insurance, travel health
Each category gets one line on what it means in practice, not a feature list. A category with no signed partner yet reads as direction, not as a working connection. No logo, no booking button, no city named for anything that isn't real yet.
About page
Why this exists: every wearable today gives you a number & leaves you there. This one is built to close that gap. What's different: acting on the data is harder to copy than just reporting on it, because it needs real relationships with the people on the other end, the doctor, the lab, the pharmacy.
One decision still open: whether this page stays scoped to just the ring & the companion, or mentions Sevyn8's broader work for credibility. Both are reasonable, this is your call to make.
Pricing & business model
One bundled price for the ring & the companion together, not hardware plus a free app. An introductory price at launch, moving up once there's customer proof. No subscription at launch. Oura & Whoop can charge one because they've already earned years of trust & a large user base, Sevyn8 does not have that yet.
Technical requirements
A working buy flow & checkout. Links to the app store once the app is live. The site needs to work well on phones, since most visitors will open it there. The hero visual needs to load fast, since it's the first thing anyone sees.

Rule for the developer
Nothing on the live site can claim more than what's true on the day it goes live. If something isn't ready, it gets one plain sentence, not a styled section built around it.
